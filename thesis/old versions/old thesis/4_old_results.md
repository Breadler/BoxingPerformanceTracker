CHAPTER 4
RESULT AND DISCUSSION
4.1	Introduction
This chapter presents the current progress of the research and discusses the preliminary outcomes achieved up to this stage of the project. As this work is being conducted in phases, the chapter does not yet report full system implementation results or final performance evaluation. Instead, it documents the completion of the foundational research activities, including literature review, pilot study, user requirement analysis, and methodology formulation.
At this stage, the project has successfully achieved Research Objective (i), which focuses on investigating existing pose-estimation systems, identifying suitable feature representations for boxing, and understanding user needs through a pilot study. The findings from these activities directly inform the proposed system design and development plan presented in Chapter 3. The project is currently transitioning toward Research Objective (ii), which involves the development of the mobile application prototype integrating pose estimation, machine learning classification, and visualization.

4.2	Completion of Foundational Studies
The first research objective aimed to establish a strong conceptual and technical foundation for the project. This has been achieved through three key activities: literature review, pilot study, and requirement analysis.
The literature review, presented in Chapter 2, examined existing work in camera-based pose estimation, sports biomechanics, boxing-specific motion analysis, and machine learning methods such as Random Forests. It confirmed that CNN-based frameworks like MediaPipe BlazePose can extract reliable 3D joint landmarks using consumer-grade cameras. However, it also revealed a gap in mobile applications that analyze boxing performance across entire sessions using time-based trends and intuitive visualizations. These findings justified the need for a camera-only, mobile-focused solution.
In parallel, a pilot study was conducted using an online questionnaire distributed among university students, fitness enthusiasts, and boxing communities. Results showed that many athletes train regularly but lack affordable coaching or systematic feedback. While some rely on manual video review, there was strong interest in automated analysis, 3D replays, and graphical summaries of performance trends. Users expressed particular interest in tracking technique degradation and fatigue indicators over time rather than receiving only single-event feedback.
Based on these findings, clear user requirements were identified: automated pose-based analysis without wearable sensors, visual feedback through 3D skeleton replay, session-level trend graphs, and an interface accessible to both trained athletes and casual users. Together, these activities fulfill Research Objective (i) and provide the empirical and theoretical basis for system development.
4.3	Definition of the Proposed Methodology
After completing the foundational studies, a comprehensive methodology was defined and detailed in Chapter 3. It adopts a prototyping-based framework, enabling iterative refinement of pose extraction, feature computation, classification, and visualization based on testing and feedback.
The system pipeline begins with video capture via a smartphone camera, followed by 3D pose landmark extraction using MediaPipe BlazePose. These landmarks serve two purposes: powering the 3D skeleton visualization and generating engineered features for classification and trend analysis. A Random Forest classifier was chosen for detecting guard position, punch events, and movement states due to its robustness, interpretability, and suitability for small to medium datasets.
A visualization plan was also established, featuring a rigged 3D skeleton model, timeline-based playback, and synchronized performance graphs. This methodology provides the foundation for Research Objective (ii), which focuses on system development and integration.
4.4	Progress Toward Next Research Objective
The project is now transitioning to Research Objective (ii): developing the mobile application prototype. The system architecture, model training plan, and visualization workflow have been fully specified, while implementation and integration are in progress.
Upcoming development steps include:
•	Completing large-scale landmark extraction and labeling for training data
•	Training and optimizing the Random Forest classification model
•	Integrating the trained model into an Android application using TensorFlow Lite
•	Implementing the 3D skeleton viewer, timeline playback, and performance graphs
These steps will enable the system to deliver meaningful visual summaries of boxing performance across entire training sessions.

4.5	Summary
This chapter documented the current progress of the research and discussed the preliminary findings achieved to date. Research Objective (i) has been successfully completed through a comprehensive literature review, pilot study, and user requirement analysis. These efforts established the feasibility, relevance, and necessity of a mobile, AI-driven boxing performance tracking system.
Furthermore, a complete methodology and system architecture have been defined, and preliminary data collection and pose extraction tests have validated the technical assumptions of the project. The research is now progressing toward Research Objective (ii), focusing on prototype development and system integration.
