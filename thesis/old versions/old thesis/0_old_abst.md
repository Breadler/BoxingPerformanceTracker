This thesis presents the design and development of a mobile application for boxing performance analysis using AI-driven 3D pose estimation and Random Forest classification to address limitations in traditional training methods that rely on manual observation or costly hardware. The research begins with a comprehensive review of pose estimation and machine learning applications in sports biomechanics, identifying gaps in existing solutions such as the absence of session-level fatigue tracking and posture visualization. A pilot study involving 40 respondents validates strong user demand for posture tracking, 3D replays, and trend analytics, confirming the feasibility of a camera-only approach. The proposed methodology adopts an iterative prototyping framework comprising data acquisition, landmark extraction, feature engineering, model development, and visualization integration. Video data from public boxing datasets and self-recorded sessions are processed using MediaPipe BlazePose to extract 33 three-dimensional landmarks per frame, which are transformed into engineered features representing guard height, punch motion vectors, and movement variance. A Random Forest classifier is trained on these features to detect guard position, punch events, and movement states, chosen for its robustness to noisy inputs and suitability for mobile deployment via TensorFlow Lite. The system architecture integrates modules for video capture, pose extraction, feature computation, classification, and synchronized visualization, including a rigged 3D skeleton viewer and time-series graphs to provide interpretable feedback on posture trends and fatigue-related degradation across entire training sessions. By combining lightweight AI models with mobile-friendly visualization, this research delivers a cost-effective, camera-only solution for boxing performance analysis, addressing critical gaps in accessibility and usability while maintaining technical rigor through feature-based classification and modular design. Future work could extend the system to real-time feedback, additional boxing metrics, and multi-sport applications, establishing a foundation for AI-driven athletic performance monitoring on consumer devices.
