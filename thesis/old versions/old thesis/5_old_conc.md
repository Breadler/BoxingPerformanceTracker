CHAPTER 5
CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FUTURE RESEARCH
5.1	Introduction
This chapter concludes the research by revisiting the aims, summarising how the proposed methodology addresses the research objectives, and outlining the contributions made to the field of mobile pose-based performance analysis.
5.2	Summary of the Research Objectives
The main objective of this study was to design a complete methodological framework for a mobile-based AI boxing analysis system that uses MediaPipe pose estimation, Random Forest classification, and a 3D visualisation engine to track guard stability, punch events, and movement frequency across a training session.

v)	To develop a mobile application prototype that combines video capture, AI‑based pose estimation, and machine learning classification to track boxing movements and fatigue, then present the results through intuitive visualizations for the user.
vi)	To evaluate the system’s effectiveness and usability by validating against a self-collected smartphone boxing dataset and user feedback.
5.3	Research Contributions
This thesis introduces a unified methodology for camera-only 3D boxing analytics, addressing limitations of existing systems that rely on hardware or 2D metrics. The proposed framework combines MediaPipe-based pose estimation, feature engineering, and machine learning classification into a mobile-friendly pipeline, enabling accessible and cost-effective performance analysis without specialized equipment.
A key contribution is the application of Random Forests for fine-grained boxing state recognition. Unlike prior approaches that use threshold logic or computationally heavy deep learning models, this method should efficiently classifies guard position, punch events, and movement frequency. It achieves a balance between accuracy, interpretability, and mobile compatibility, making it suitable for real-world deployment.
Finally, the research establishes a workflow for 3D pose timeline replay on mobile devices, integrating skeleton animation with synchronized performance graphs. This is an innovation absent in current literature. Insights from a pilot study validated the inclusion of features such as fatigue tracking, 3D replays, and time-series analytics, ensuring the system aligns with user needs for affordable, camera-only tools.

5.4	Pratical Implications and Beneficiaries
The proposed methods offer practical benefits across multiple user groups. For boxers and combat athletes, the system provides objective performance insights without requiring a coach or expensive hardware, making advanced training analysis accessible in personal settings. Similarly, general fitness users can leverage 3D replays and trend graphs to monitor form degradation and improve technique, enhancing workout quality without specialized equipment.
Coaches and training facilities can use the tool for asynchronous athlete monitoring, visual cueing, and evidence-based training plans. This enables remote feedback and structured performance tracking, reducing reliance on in-person sessions while maintaining training effectiveness.
Finally, the methodology benefits mobile developers and AI practitioners by offering a replicable framework that combines MediaPipe pose estimation, Random Forest classification, and 3D visualization within a mobile application. This approach can serve as a foundation for future sports analytics tools or extensions into other fitness domains.

5.5	Limitions of The Present Study
This study faces several limitations due to its scope and development stage. First, the system depends on MediaPipe’s landmark detection quality, which may degrade under challenging conditions such as occlusions, rapid movements, or poor lighting. Second, the diversity of boxing data is limited; while multiple datasets were used, they may not fully represent variations in fighting styles, stances, and camera perspectives. Finally, although real-time speed was not a primary objective, the application may require optimization to ensure consistent performance across different mobile devices.
5.6	Future Works
Building on the foundation established in this thesis, future research can expand the system’s capabilities in several directions. First, additional boxing metrics such as punch accuracy, guard recovery time, head movement, stance stability, and timing can be incorporated to provide more comprehensive performance analysis. Second, real-time feedback features could be introduced, enabling live detection of guard drops, audio cues for corrections, and round tracking during active sessions. Finally, the pose-based pipeline can be extended beyond boxing to other sports, including karate, taekwondo, MMA, and general fitness, offering a versatile framework for movement quality assessment across multiple disciplines.
5.7	Summary
This chapter summarised the main contributions of the thesis and outlined the implications, limitations, and directions for future work. Although the practical implementation phase is still ahead, the research has established a strong theoretical and methodological foundation for developing a camera-only, AI-driven boxing analysis application with 3D visualisation and temporal trend analytics. The next stage will translate the proposed methodology into a functional system that can be empirically evaluated and iteratively improved based on user testing and performance metrics.
